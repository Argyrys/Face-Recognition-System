1. Problem Statement & Objective

A VC (Vice-Chancellor) cabin entry currently has no reliable record of who entered and when. Manual register entries are slow, inaccurate and easy to forge. The objective is to build a system that identifies students and faculty at the entry point automatically — and that behaves like a real security appliance rather than a demo: decisions rest on accumulated evidence, attacks are detected, failures are visible, and every action is auditable.

Scope boundary (important): this prototype is software only. It detects a face, decides who it is, records it, and raises an alert. It never opens a door or controls any lock — the security guard / staff member reviews the alert and physically grants or denies access.

Objectives

  • Detect and track faces reliably at the entry point — multiple visitors simultaneously, stable identities across frames
  • Recognize enrolled students and faculty on sustained evidence (N-frame confirmation), not a single lucky frame
  • Reject presentation attacks — printed photos and frozen screens must never be accepted as a live person
  • Log every entry with identity, department, ID, time, result and liveness verdict
  • Notify staff instantly — on screen, and out-of-band by email or webhook when configured
  • Stay available: detect camera failures and recover automatically
  • Keep a complete, tamper-evident audit trail; keep data local and short-lived
  • Work with or without a physical webcam (video, images, or phone camera)

2. System Architecture

The system runs entirely on one machine. A background thread pulls frames through a six-stage recognition pipeline, a Flask server (Waitress in production) serves the login-gated dashboard, and everything is stored in SQLite.

4. How the Recognition Pipeline Works

Capture a frame. From the configured source (webcam, video, image folder, or phone camera over Wi-Fi).
Detect all faces (YuNet). Every face in the frame is processed independently — the system handles multiple visitors at once. Frames with no face are skipped silently.
Track across frames (IoU tracker). Each face gets a stable track id; detections are attached to existing tracks by intersection-over-union, so one visitor is one track, not a stream of unrelated frames.
Embed the face (SFace). The face is aligned using its 5 landmarks, then encoded into an L2-normalized 128-D vector and matched against the gallery by cosine similarity.
N-frame confirmation. A MATCHED or UNKNOWN verdict requires 3 consecutive frames agreeing (VC_CONFIRM_FRAMES). One blurred or lucky frame can no longer create or deny a log entry.
Liveness gate. Landmark motion and blink energy are accumulated per track. On live sources, a confirmed match that is not verified live is held (up to 20 frames) and then logged unverified; a photo/screen attack is logged as a presentation attack and raises a critical alert.
Log the entry. Name, category, department, ID, timestamp, confidence, result and liveness verdict are written to entry_log with a cropped snapshot.
Notify staff. Desktop toast + dashboard alert, and email/webhook when configured. Staff then Acknowledge or Reject.

Live workflow visibility

The dashboard shows the pipeline decision process in real time. Each tracked face is rendered as a stage strip — DETECT → TRACK → CONFIRM → LIVENESS → MATCH → LOG — green when a stage passed, cyan while evidence is accruing, amber while holding, red-blinking on a spoof verdict. This makes the ~1 second confirmation window visible instead of looking like a malfunction, and shows the operator exactly why an entry has (or has not) been logged.

Duplicate suppression

A 15-second cooldown (VC_COOLDOWN) prevents the same person (or the same unknown visitor) from being logged repeatedly while they stand in front of the camera. The cooldown is keyed per identity, so two different visitors are never throttled against each other.

When a face is not recognized

The entry is logged as UNKNOWN with the raw cosine score — never force-matched to the nearest enrolled person. Unknowns get the same N-frame treatment as matches, so detection flicker cannot spam the alert list.

5. Recognition Security: Evidence, Not One-Shot Decisions

Threshold sweep — an evidenced operating point

The SFace vendor default (0.363) is a reasonable start, but it is not a measurement on this camera or this gallery. threshold_sweep.py measures score distributions — genuine pairs (same identity across augmented cross-condition variants: flip, rotation, brightness, blur, scale) versus imposter pairs (every cross-identity combination) — and sweeps the threshold from 0.20 to 0.70, reporting FAR, FRR, precision, recall and F1 at each point.

Every threshold in [0.21, 0.69] achieved zero false accepts at full recall on the measured pairs; the recommendation is the interval midpoint (largest margin from both the nearest imposter and the nearest genuine score). The vendor default 0.363 sits inside the proven-safe interval, so it was kept as the shipped default — now as an evidenced choice, not an assumption. The sweep is operator-tunable (VC_MIN_COSINE) and re-runnable on-site with real probe photos.

Honest limitation: augmented variants are a surrogate for a real second capture session, and 2 identities cannot bound a production FAR. The generated report says so, and instructs re-running the sweep with probes from the actual installation camera before freezing the threshold.

Passive liveness (anti-spoofing)

Per track, two cheap signals are computed from the ordinary RGB feed:

  • Landmark motion — frame-to-frame displacement of the 5 YuNet landmarks, normalized by face size (scale- and distance-invariant);
  • Blink detection — Laplacian texture energy of the two eye crops relative to the whole face (lighting-invariant). A blink drops the energy sharply below the personal baseline and back.

A printed photo or frozen screen shows neither motion nor blinks and is judged a spoof — but only after ≥10 frames of evidence, so a genuinely still visitor is never branded an attacker on 3 frames. In enforced mode (the default on live sources) a spoof verdict never becomes MATCHED, even for an enrolled person; it is logged as a presentation attack with a critical alert. Modes: enforced | monitor (label only) | off.

Honest limitation: passive single-camera checks cannot distinguish a high-quality video replay of a blinking person from the real person. The threat model (docs/SECURITY.md) documents this and recommends a discretionary guard challenge (ask the visitor to turn their head) for higher-risk installations — which the live workflow view and camera feed make easy to supervise.

6. Dashboard Features

Access control

Every page and API (except /login, /health and static assets) sits behind a single admin password (VC_ADMIN_PASSWORD; generated randomly and printed to the log if unset — the dashboard is never left open). Five failed attempts trigger a 60-second per-IP lockout; sessions are HttpOnly/SameSite cookies with a 12-hour lifetime; cross-site mutating requests are refused (CSRF defence).

Two ways to use it

  • Live monitoring — point a webcam (or a phone camera acting as one) at the entry; the dashboard shows the feed, the workflow strip, and logs people as they pass.
  • Upload & analyze — drop in a still image and get an instant identity verdict. Useful when no camera is available.

Notification handling

Each alert can be Acknowledged (handled/allowed) or Rejected (denied). Both actions clear the notification and record the decision for audit. If nobody acts on an alert, it clears itself after 24 hours, so the notification list never grows without bound.

7. Operations: Alerts, Watchdog, Backups, Hardening

Out-of-band alert routing

On-screen alerts only help someone who is watching. alerter.py routes events to an SMTP mailbox and/or a generic webhook (Discord/Slack/IFTTT/Zapier-compatible), configured purely by environment variables. Delivery is rate-limited per channel (VC_ALERT_MIN_INTERVAL, default 60 s) so a noisy stream cannot flood the guard, while critical events always deliver — suspected spoofing and camera outages. A failing channel (bad credentials, network down) is logged and never crashes the pipeline. With no channels configured, behavior is exactly as before.

Camera watchdog

A security camera that silently stops is worse than none. The watchdog watches frame flow on live sources: no frames for 25 seconds (VC_STALL_SECONDS) triggers an automatic pipeline restart with backoff, a red "camera outage — entry unmonitored" banner on the dashboard, camera.outage / camera.recovered audit entries, and a critical alert. The degraded state is visible rather than silent.

Backups

python backup.py produces a timestamped, compressed archive of the database (via SQLite's backup API — consistent even while the app is writing) plus all snapshot images and a manifest with row counts and restore steps. Old archives are pruned (VC_BACKUP_KEEP, default 30); --verify integrity-checks the live database. The README ships Task Scheduler (Windows) and cron (Linux) recipes for daily backups.

Transport & hardening

  • Security headers on every response: X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, X-Frame-Options, Referrer-Policy; API responses are no-store.
  • HTTPS by reverse proxy: the production server (Waitress) deliberately does not terminate TLS itself — ready-to-use Caddy (automatic certificates) and nginx configurations are documented, with the app bound to localhost behind the proxy.
  • Retention: entries, alerts and snapshots purged after 30 days, audit rows after 90 — automatic, on startup and every 6 hours.
  • 45 automated tests (pytest, no camera needed) + GitHub Actions CI on every push (Python 3.11 & 3.12); Docker image with health check.

8. Live Camera — Phone as a Webcam

The built-in webcam on this machine was not available, so the system was built to accept a phone camera as a live source. Running an app such as IP Webcam or DroidCam on the phone exposes the camera as an MJPEG video stream over Wi-Fi, which the system consumes directly.

py app.py --source http://<phone-ip>:8080/video
Engineering note: OpenCV's FFMPEG backend buffers HTTP streams heavily — a phone camera serving ~15 fps was read at under 0.25 fps through VideoCapture. A dedicated MJPEG multipart reader (camera.MjpegStream) was written to parse the stream directly, raising throughput to ~4.3 fps at 640×480. The pipeline also auto-reconnects if the phone drops off the network, and the watchdog restarts it if frames stop altogether.

Consent-first enrollment

Enrollment requires an explicit consent checkbox. No face is enrolled without it. Face embeddings are stored as binary blobs, not photographs.

Data locality, retention and rights

  • All data lives locally: database/vc_entry.db plus snapshot images in static/snapshots/; nothing leaves the machine except operator-configured alert summaries (text only, never embeddings)
  • Automatic retention: entries/alerts/snapshots 30 days, audit 90 days
  • Data-subject rights: deactivation/deletion removes the person's embedding, photo and profile; full erasure = delete DB + snapshots + backups
  • Snapshots are capped (240 most recent) and pruned automatically

Human-in-the-loop by design

The software produces a recommendation, never an action. Every alert requires a human Acknowledge/Reject, and no physical access control is driven by the system.

Limitations and bias

  • Accuracy depends on lighting, distance and face size in frame
  • Trained primarily on adult faces — performance on very young or elderly subjects may differ
  • The enrolled gallery determines who can ever be recognized; unknown faces are never guessed
  • Liveness is passive: it stops photo/screen attacks but not a high-quality video replay (see threat model)

What this prototype does NOT do

No door control, no lock control, no automated entry grant. The prototype stops at identification, logging and notification.
